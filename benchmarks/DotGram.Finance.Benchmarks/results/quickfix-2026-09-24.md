## What was compared

| | ours | QuickFIX/n |
| --- | --- | --- |
| parse | `FixParser.ParseMessage(wire, context)` | `Message.FromString(wire, validate: false, dd, dd, factory)` |
| parse and validate | the same, then `message.Validate(context)` | the same with `validate: true`, then `DataDictionary.Validate(message, dd, dd, "FIX.4.4", msgType)` |
| parse from octets | `FixParser.ParseMessage(byte[], context)` | `Parser.AddToStream(span)`, `ReadFixMessage(out wire)`, then `FromString(wire, …)` |

Parse is paired with parse and validate with validate; their validating side is two calls because
the flag alone accepts a message missing a required field and ours does not. Every row reads the
same tags on both sides, in the same order. Five shapes: `Order` flat, `Parties` at three entries,
`PartiesMedium` at a hundred, `PartiesLarge` at a thousand, `Binary` a length/data pair.
`.work/qfcompare/basis.md` carries the whole basis, including why each call was chosen.

**The octet row gives each side its own road from a socket.** Theirs goes through `QuickFix.Parser`,
which is what their `SocketReader` holds: it cuts a whole message out of a byte stream by BodyLength
and decodes it. No encoding is passed because their default already is the right one — `Parser()`
gives `iso-8859-1`, read off `QuickFix.dll` by reflection rather than assumed — and the span overload
is taken because 1.14.1 marks `AddToStream(byte[], int)` obsolete. One parser per case, reused, as
their reader holds one per connection.

## The checks, all of which passed before anything was measured

- **`--against-check`**: every shape, both schema modes, both sides, and the octet road — the two
  sides read the same numbers in all ten cases (267, 499, 7857, 77157, 65), our findings are zero
  everywhere, and their validating pair returns rather than throwing on a valid message.
- **`--flag-check`**: asked of their library, not assumed. On a NewOrderSingle with no ClOrdID:
  ours 1 finding `RequiredFieldMissing`; their `validate:false` accepts; their `validate:true`
  **accepts**; their `validate:true` plus `DataDictionary.Validate` refuses with
  `RequiredTagMissing`. That is why their validating row is two calls.
- **`--matrix`**: below.
- **`--guard-check`: removed, not passed.** A schema used to be a static field on the message class,
  so every case had to assert which schema its process held. It is a value now — `Fix44Context` —
  handed to each call, so there is no state to guard. A check that cannot fail is worse than none.

## The defect matrix, and the two rows that changed

| defect | ours | QuickFIX/n (their pair) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| valid | accepted | accepted |
| no ClOrdID (required) | 1 finding: `RequiredFieldMissing` | refused: `RequiredTagMissing` |
| Side not in its code set | 1 finding: `InvalidValue` | refused: `IncorrectTagValue` |
| OrderQty not a number | 1 finding: `InvalidValue` | refused: `IncorrectDataFormat` |
| a tag the schema does not place here | 1 finding: `FieldNotInScope` | refused: `TagNotDefinedForMessage` |
| **CheckSum wrong** | **1 finding: `CheckSumMismatch`** | refused: `InvalidMessage` (CheckSum) |
| **BodyLength wrong** | **1 finding: `BodyLengthMismatch`** | refused: `InvalidMessage` (BodyLength) |

**The two framing rows moved from reading to validation.** Until 2026-09-21 a wrong BodyLength or
CheckSum was a refusal of the read — a `FormatException` before a message existed. They are findings
of `Validate` now: the message is built, and the framing is reported as wrong. A consumer who parses
and does not validate therefore reads a message whose framing does not add up, where before they
were stopped at the door. On the seven defects neither side does work the other does not; the form
differs, ours answering with every finding and theirs throwing on the first.

Two notes about the witnesses, because a witness that carries two defects reports two:

- the out-of-scope wire carries tag 31, which the standard defines and does not place in a
  NewOrderSingle — a sharper witness than an undefined tag, and their answer names the row
  (`TagNotDefinedForMessage`) rather than `InvalidTagNumber`;
- the BodyLength wire **recomputes its checksum**. Without that the digits move, the old checksum no
  longer matches, and the row reports a second finding the harness introduced.

And one consequence found by the encoding probe rather than reasoned about: a wire whose octets were
decoded as UTF-8 used to be refused with a message about BodyLength. It is now **read**, with the
non-ASCII value one character short, and only `Validate` says the framing is wrong. The misleading
refusal became a silent wrong read for anyone who does not validate.

## Allocations, bytes a message

Cores 16-31, `GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread` over a hundred messages after a warm-up — a
second instrument beside `MemoryDiagnoser` rather than a repetition of it. Compiled schema; the
loaded one is in the next table but one.

| `Parties` | 3,800 | 11,088 | 2.92 | 3,800 | 14,696 | 3.87 |
| `PartiesMedium` | 30,760 | 198,704 | 6.46 | 30,760 | 250,432 | 8.14 |

**Our validation allocates nothing.** Parse and parse-plus-validate are the same number at every
shape — 2,912 and 2,912, 275,568 and 275,568 — because on a valid message no finding is built and
there is no list to hold them. Theirs costs 2,064 bytes on an `Order` and 498,102 on a thousand
entries.

**The ratio is not one number: it grows with the group.** 1.65× on a flat order and 7.02× at a
thousand entries. Per entry, across the 997 that separate `Parties` from `PartiesLarge`: **ours 273
bytes an entry, theirs 1,929** — seven times, which is where the flat-message figure goes when the
message stops being flat. Two sizes would have given a price; three show it is the group and not the
message.

Ours costs **+56 bytes** over the string road at every shape — the materialised source, one
character to one octet — and theirs costs between 296 and 50,104 more, because their parser copies
the bytes into its own buffer and decodes them. The octet road is not cheaper on either side; on
ours it is the road that makes no claim about who decoded the wire.

### The resolution of an allocation figure

The two schema modes are identical work for their side, so the two cases are an A/A. Theirs differs
by **144 bytes** between them at four of five shapes; ours by **0** at three and 32 at two. So a
difference under about 150 bytes on their side is not a difference, and the figures above are three
to four orders of magnitude larger than that.

Our own two modes allocate the same: reading with a loaded dictionary costs what reading with the
compiled schema costs, because a context is consulted, not copied.

### What may and may not be said from this

**Quotable.** Parsing is 1.5× to 2.2× on the four shapes where the effect clears the resolution, and
the ratio rises with the group rather than being one number. Parsing and validating is 2.6× to 3.5×
at every shape including `Binary`. Those are eight rows whose effect is three to a hundred times the
A/A spread.

**Not quotable, and marked as such.** `Binary` parse (1.29) and two of the octet rows (`Order` 1.23,
`Binary` 1.15) sit inside or barely outside the A/A spread of the same run. They are printed because
leaving them out would be selecting, and they are not effects.

**Nor is our own octet road slower than our string road**, although the numbers read that way: +8%
on `Order`, +4% on `PartiesLarge`, −5% on `Binary`, all inside the spread. Only `Parties` (+22%
against 2%) and `PartiesMedium` (+20% against 5%) clear it, and those two say the octet road costs
about a fifth more on a grouped message — the materialised source, which the allocation table prices
at +56 bytes.

**The allocation columns of this run agree with the dedicated allocation run** — 2.84 KB against
2,912 bytes on `Order`, 269.11 KB against 275,568 — which is two instruments agreeing about the same
thing after a day's work in between.

## Reading the dictionary, cold: the one row that goes the other way

One invocation a sample, fresh processes, the file read once first so that neither side pays the
disk for both. Medians, because both sides are skewed on a machine this busy.

| | ours | QuickFIX/n |
|---|---|---|
| time, median | **697.92 ms** | **20.56 ms** |
| time, mean ± SD | 690.51 ± 36.30 ms | 27.23 ± 21.47 ms |
| allocated | **252.49 MB** | **4.85 MB** |

**Thirty-four times their time and fifty-two times their memory, and the reason is what the load
buys.** Their `DataDictionary` builds tables to be walked. Our `Load` compiles the file's checks
through the expression language, so the cost of every later message is what the tables above show
and the cost of the load is this. It is paid once per process, by a consumer who holds a venue's
dictionary rather than the compiled-in schema — and it is the largest single figure in this file,
which is why it is not left to a footnote.

**This figure is of the load as it was on the day, and the mechanism changed the next day.** Since
`e2d8204e` (2026-09-25) a dictionary's checks compile when one is first asked, not when the dictionary
is applied, so a consumer who loads a venue's file no longer pays all of this at the load. What the
split costs was not re-measured: the row above stands as taken, and nothing here says what it would
read today. What the first ASK costs is measured, in
`benchmarks/results/stand-fixmsg-versions-2026-09-25.md`: the stand's loaded reading compiles its
checks inside its warm-up, so no timed row carries them.

**And their file does not load into this package on its own.** `Fix44Context.Default.Load` of
`FIX44.xml` alone is refused:

    The dictionary describes 'QuoteRequestReject_NoQuoteQualifiers', which this package has no slot for.

so the row reads their file **and** the errata this repository keeps beside it. Their row reads one
file, ours reads two — not because ours was given more, but because ours will not read theirs alone.
A row that quietly loaded the errata and said nothing would be hiding a refusal.
