Does a failing turn's log come back the same? (D63's correctness question)

A turn that fails restores the log to a mark, and the next attempt writes into the same
place. If what it writes were always what was discarded, a replay could keep the records
rather than make them again. The answer is neither yes nor no: nothing comes back
identical, because a give-back reads one turn fewer and so the tail must differ - but
52% to 91% of each discarded segment is written back unchanged, and THE SHARE GROWS WITH
THE INPUT, which is the quadratic seen from the log instead of from the call counts.

THE SHARE GROWS WITH THE INPUT, and that is stronger than any single row: 57 to 73 per cent
for a media type, 52 to 71 for a language tag, 84 to 92 for a structured field, as the input
goes from sixteen units to sixty-four. A lengthening identical prefix against a differing tail
of about constant size is the same quadratic the call counts show, appearing here in a second
instrument that measures something else entirely. That is how a disease is told from an
artefact of one instrument: it shows up where the instrument was not built to look for it.

Revision: 4e541d8a, on top of origin/main e48ab0aa. Counts, not times: the instrumented
build is slower and only its counts mean anything.

HOW IT IS KEYED, and three ways it was keyed wrongly first - each produced a confident
wrong answer, so they are recorded rather than tidied away:

  1. By the log mark alone. Every difference reported 'first at 0', which is where every
     top-level attempt begins, so unrelated turns sharing an offset were compared.
  2. By mark and position, with the hook gated to methods declaring 'var p = pos;'. That
     dropped 249 roll-backs - and give-backs happen in the way-back wrappers, which was
     exactly what the gate excluded. Media type had 1 of its 140 roll-backs covered, and
     reported 'none differ'.
  3. By whole-segment equality, which calls everything different. The tail must differ;
     the question is how much of the head does not.

The key that answers it: the log mark, plus the moving position inside a reader or the
entry position inside a way-back wrapper - the wrapper being re-entered at the same place
each time a way is retried is what makes it the same place.
