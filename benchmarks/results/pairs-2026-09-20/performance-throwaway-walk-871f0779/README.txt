HOW TO READ IT (the architect's caveat, stated before the run): the throwaway measures the cost of the SECOND walk, over tables the first walk has just brought into the cache. That is a LOWER bound of what the walk costs once, so the answer reads "the walk costs AT LEAST this much", not "this is what removing it would save". What the run supports: the second walk alone costs about 49 ns on a parse of one literal, at least the 43 ns that the counting run attributed to the fixed part, so the walk is enough to account for that whole fixed part; that the FIRST walk costs more, and that removing the walk would save at least that much on a real fix, are expectations and were not measured (a removal would have to be a correct change: performance-9f's decision). What is NOT established: what makes up the rest of the 66 ns gap on literal between the generated parser (80 ns) and the hand-written one (13.8 ns) in BDN (this run says a walk of the size of about 49 ns is present in it, not that nothing else is).
