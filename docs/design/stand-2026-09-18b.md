# The stand, every family: 2026-09-18, evening

The first baseline to carry every family the repository has a parser for: FIX, the web's
formats, T-SQL against Microsoft's ScriptDom, the expression language and SQL:2023, each
against its hand-written parser where there is one and against the reference outside the
repository where there is not, and a regular expression where one can be written honestly.
Raw data: [`stand-2026-09-18b.json`](../../benchmarks/results/stand-2026-09-18b.json) and its
[`.md`](../../benchmarks/results/stand-2026-09-18b.md), the twin taken with tiered PGO off:
[`pgo0-stand-2026-09-18.json`](../../benchmarks/results/pgo0-stand-2026-09-18.json) and
[`pgo0-compare-2026-09-18.md`](../../benchmarks/results/pgo0-compare-2026-09-18.md). The
morning's [baseline](stand-2026-09-18.md) is what the generator's time is held to below.

## How it was taken

`--stand --repeat 5`, 21:04 to 21:30, from binaries built at `2d9bd3b6` (main) plus the
`DOTGRAM_ROOT` override, on logical processors 0-15 at high priority, every other session's
builds pinned to 16-31. Five runs, each in a process of its own; the report is each reading's
median. The controls of the five (arithmetic hashing, which moves when the machine does):
31.7, 31.4, 31.9, 31.5, 31.3 ns, so no run was dropped. The **spread** column is the base
reading's spread *between the five runs*, not between rounds: it is what a change is to be
held against, and it is above 20% on a few rows named at the end. A reading's ratio is its
time over the row's base; the base is the hand-written parser, and where there is none the
generated one (`web/addr-spec`, `media-type`, `sf`) or ScriptDom (`tsql`).

The rows are timed with the runtime's defaults, which include dynamic PGO; the twin below is
the same set with `DOTNET_TieredPGO=0`, three runs, and it moves more than expected.

**The regular expression is not a floor.** `(\d+)=([^\x01]*)\x01` does the split into tag and
value and nothing else — no typed value, no length/data pair, no recovery — and it costs
1.3-2.1x the hand parser, about what the generated one costs (2.1-2.5x): compiled it is 1.0-1.7x.
The hand parser does more than the pattern for less time; a regular expression buys nothing here
except its allocation, which is 5-8x the parsers' (742,728 B against 95,408 for `Orders128`).

## T-SQL against ScriptDom

ScriptDom (`TSql170Parser`, the tree of one statement) is the base; the generated parser
without positions and with them (`located`, the one to hold against it: ScriptDom always
carries where each part was). Agreement is that all three accept the statement; that the trees
say the same is what `--roundtrip` and `--kinds` answer over the corpus.

| statement | ScriptDom ns | generated | located | ScriptDom B | generated B |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| select-join | 31,263 | 0.28x | 0.42x | 61,582 | 3,280 |
| insert-values | 12,090 | 0.55x | 0.67x | 48,504 | 2,360 |
| create-table | 19,385 | 0.37x | 0.45x | 55,656 | 4,736 |
| update-subquery | 19,718 | 0.34x | 0.51x | 54,632 | 2,528 |
| select-long | 101,627 | 0.36x | 0.55x | 138,194 | 13,224 |

The generated parser is 1.8-3.6x faster than ScriptDom's tree (1.5-2.4x located) and allocates
one tenth to one twentieth of what it does (2.4-13.2 KB against 48.5-138 KB). `select-join` and `select-long` have ScriptDom spreads of
56-59% between runs, so their ratios are the least certain; the other three are 4-13%.
This replaces the README's "T-SQL against ScriptDom" figures, taken before this week's changes,
for the stand's rows; the corpus-wide figures there (`ScriptDomBenchmarks`, `--speed`) have
not been retaken.

## What tiered PGO does to every ratio

The same rows under `DOTNET_TieredPGO=0`, three runs, control 31.1 ns on all three. Every
reading slows down, and the hand parsers and ScriptDom slow down most:

- the median hand reading is **+38%** (from -15% to +146%: `web/url.long-path` hand 194 to 476 ns);
- the median generated / tape / immediate reading is **+23%** (from -6% to +66%);
- ScriptDom, +60..85% (the generated T-SQL parser +8..20%).

So the ratios move, and always towards the generated parser: `fix/One.text` 2.54x becomes 2.27x,
`web/url.plain` 1.72x becomes 1.03x, `sql/literal` 8.0x becomes 5.9x, `tsql/insert-values`
0.55x of ScriptDom becomes 0.32x, `el/floor` tape 2.54x becomes 2.15x; 79 of the 127 readings'
ratios move by 10% or more. The hand parsers profit from dynamic PGO more than the generated code
does. Every ratio in this document, and in the
architecture decisions that quote one, is therefore *with default tiered PGO*; the twin is what to
read a ratio against when someone asks how much of it is the profile. A harness that checked
agreement on other inputs than it timed read the generated FIX parser at 185 ns a field where the
stand had 140 for the same reason, which is why the stand's own agreement check runs over
every row before any is timed, and why this twin is taken.

## What is not settled

- Spread above 20% between the five runs: `el/interpolation` 52% (the hand reading is the
  unstable one), `el/refused-early` 45%, `sql/literal` 44% (20 ns hand), `tsql/select-join`
  59% and `select-long` 56% (ScriptDom), `fix/slope-4` 22%, `sql/column` 22%, `sql/values` 20%.
  A change on those rows must be larger than that to be believed.
- The corpus-wide T-SQL figures (`ScriptDomBenchmarks`, `--speed`) are the README's, from
  before the week; the stand's `tsql/*` rows are five statements, not the corpus.
- Regular expressions are N/A for SQL, the expression language, RFC 8259 and RFC 9651.
- The feeds family (`feeds/stock-count`) and the first-call anatomy tools are not in this run.
