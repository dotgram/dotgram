# The stand, every family with its regular expressions: 2026-09-19, afternoon

The third baseline, taken for Igor's report at the architect's request: every family the repository has a
parser for, each against its hand-written parser (or, where there is none, against the reference outside the
repository or the generated one), with the regular expressions beside them, the PGO-off twin, and the
change against this morning's baseline ([`stand-2026-09-19`](stand-2026-09-19.md), at `8b0a3b89`) and against the
evening of the 18th ([`stand-2026-09-18b`](stand-2026-09-18b.md)). Raw data:
[`stand-2026-09-19b.json`](../../benchmarks/results/stand-2026-09-19b.json) and
[`.md`](../../benchmarks/results/stand-2026-09-19b.md), the twin
[`pgo0-stand-2026-09-19b-two-runs.json`](../../benchmarks/results/pgo0-stand-2026-09-19b-two-runs.json), and
[`linearity-2026-09-19b.md`](../../benchmarks/results/linearity-2026-09-19b.md).

## How it was taken

`--stand --repeat 5`, 14:09 to 14:39, from binaries built at `841ce7c7` (every commit of main up to 14:08), on
logical processors 0-15 at high priority, every other session's builds pinned to 16-31. The controls of the five:
32.0, 31.9, 32.1, 31.6, 31.9 ns, so no run was dropped. **The PGO-off twin is weaker than the base**: three runs
(14:39 to 14:55) with controls 36.0, 31.0, 31.4 ns; the gate dropped the first, and fewer than three clean runs is
below the rule's minimum, so the pooled figure is the mean of two runs and is a twin of the shape, not a median to
quote to a per cent. A rerun of five is queued. Every ratio below is at the runtime's default tiered PGO; the PGO=0
column is a twin and is not mixed with them.

The base of a row is the hand-written parser (FIX, URL, date-time, JSON, the expression language, SQL:2023, the
stock count); ScriptDom for T-SQL; the generated parser where there is none (address, media type, structured
fields, cookie, JSON Pointer). A ratio is a reading's time over the base's. **The regular expressions are
transcriptions of what the parser does, and each does less**: for FIX the two "lesser" readings split tag from
value and do nothing else (no typed value, no length/data pair, no recovery); for the web the patterns are the
specification's grammar without the value the parser builds. So a regex above the generated parser's time is a
regex that costs more for doing less.

## What moved since the morning

- **FIX text**: the generated parser went from 1.72x of the hand parser to 0.76x, that is 56% less, and reads FIX
  slopes at 0.73x: it is faster than the hand parser on strings now. The bytes forms went 1.81x -> 1.71x (-5%), the
  stream forms 2.63x -> 2.19x over 11 rows (the slope streams are new; over the five they had, 2.63x -> 2.54x).
  This is what the pairs of the plain machine on the immediate carrier read (-30% text in performance-ff's
  experiment A, -45..-60% with C; -41..-56% on the bytes forms in C4c step 1).
- **JSON** 3.02x -> 1.38x and **date-time** 6.89x -> 2.32x (-54% and -66%): sql-39's 81320649 read -49% and -58%
  on JSON in its pair; performance-ff names 4f4d4d37 for RFC 3339's spans.
- **The expression language's tape** 2.18x -> 1.98x (-9%): the quadratic of 584a7c1f is gone and the tape at a
  thousand terms reads 150 us (1,322 in the morning), see linearity below; immediate 1.39x -> 1.36x.
- **SQL:2023** 7.88x -> 7.31x (-7%), 10.54x on the 18th (-28%); **T-SQL** against ScriptDom unchanged (0.32x,
  0.45x located), **URL** unchanged (2.61x -> 2.60x). **The stock count** 1.20x -> 1.07x (-11%): its text form 1.22x
  -> 0.86x, the reader forms 1.11-1.48x.

## The regular expressions

FIX text: `regex-lesser` 2.01x and compiled 1.64x of the hand parser, against the generated parser's 0.76x: the
regular expression that does the split alone costs 2.6x what the generated parser costs for the whole read, and its
allocation is 5-8x (`fix/Order.text` 5,960 B against 800). URL: the pattern is 10.7x the hand parser interpreted and
3.25x compiled (the generated parser 2.6x); date-time: 6.0x and 5.0x against the generated 2.3x, and on the refused
timestamp the regex is 1.2x against the generated 3.6x, the one place a regex reads less (it stops at the first
mismatch and builds nothing). Address 2.2x / 1.4x, media type 2.0x / 1.6x of the generated parser.

## Linearity

Flagged above 1.2: the stock count's hand parsers (GC: linear allocation, generation 2 at the largest size), JSON
object generated 1.57 and hand 1.46 (GC). The expression language's tape is 0.48 / 0.78 from 10 to 1,000 terms (it was
1.46 in the morning, the quadratic of 584a7c1f, fixed in 7cb9afba and 7937b564), immediate 0.43 / 0.94; SQL:2023
conditions 0.81 / 0.78 (linear allocation), T-SQL series 0.58-0.99. No algorithmic flag.
